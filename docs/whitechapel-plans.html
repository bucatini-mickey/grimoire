Whitechapel: three clockwork towns

Each plan is drawn as the game draws it, one character a metre. Every one passes the game's own map checks: fifteen benches in the open, a council table for sixteen indoors, a home for every player, and every door reachable. The yellow words are the map's place names. They run from grounded to fully steampunk: A keeps the clockwork to Abel Quill's shop, B makes it the town's trade, C lets it take the sky.

A · The Rookery

72 × 60 m · 4,320 m²

The East End as it was, with the clockwork kept to Abel Quill's. The railway crosses the north on a brick viaduct. Its arches are let as a smithy, a stable, a cooper's, a rag shop and lodgings, and the station stair climbs to the line. Commercial Square has Christ Church on its west side, the Ten Bells on its north-east corner and Abel Quill's facing it. Petticoat Lane's barrows run east down Wentworth Street. There is the doss-house, rows of beds in one room, and on Flower & Dean Street the houses back onto Wentworth Court and its pump. Back-to-backs line Thrawl Street, sharing a back wall with the row on the Back Alley, with a tunnel through to it.

B · The Works

76 × 64 m · 4,864 m²

Whitechapel as a clockwork town. Quill & Sons' clockworks fill the north-west: a shop floor of lathes, gear-cutting wheels and anvils, a brass furnace, a drawing office upstairs and a tall chimney. The Great Clock's tower stands over Quill Square, with the bell and the movement up its stair. The foundry roars to the east beside the Ten Bells, with livery stables behind. A canal cuts the town in two, with warehouses on its wharf, a lock and two bridges. South of it are the gasworks, with the great iron gasholder and the retort house, and the workers' back-to-backs on Cog Street and Spring Row, a Wesleyan chapel at their end.

  • Houses 4–6 × 5 m
  • Square 21 × 14 m

C · The Brass Spire

70 × 62 m · 4,340 m²

Whitechapel gone to brass and steam. In Orrery Square great gears turn round the clock. The airship Cassiopeia is moored to her iron mast in the Aerodrome yard: up the mast's stair and across the gangway are her cabins, her engine and the bridge with its wheel. The Boiler House has furnaces, flywheels and four funnels, with an iron gantry above. Abel Quill's Emporium of Automata stands on the square, its brass figures among the clocks. Christ Church and the Ten Bells keep their places. Cogwright Row runs back to back, and the overhead railway crosses the back street on iron pillars, reached up the station stair.
